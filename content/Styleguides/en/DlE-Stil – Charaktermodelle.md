# DlE style – Character models

## Purpose

Character models are the binding visual source for recurring figures. They must define face, build, clothing, colours and distinctive details clearly enough for new avatars, chat images and scenes to reproduce the same figure reliably.

This guideline derives from the existing reference models in `Galerie/Charaktermodelle`. The original M1 basic series uses 1024 × 1536 pixels. M2 adds a more individual world series whose format depends on the required reference coverage.

## Binding basic format of the M1 series

- Portrait format with a **2:3** ratio, preferably **1024 × 1536 pixels** or larger at exactly the same ratio.
- Dark cinematic character sheet with two clearly readable areas:
  - identity and a dominant full-body portrayal on the left,
  - factual reference modules on the right.
- The grid provides organisation and **is not a visible modern user interface**. The completed sheet resembles a curated fantasy concept board, not a dashboard or collection of uniform cards.
- Outer margin at 1024-pixel width: approximately 24–32 pixels.
- Column ratio: approximately **48 % left / 52 % right**; acceptable left-column range 45–52 %.
- Narrow, visually calm central gutter of approximately 12–20 pixels.
- All content stays within a safe margin. Horns, hair, weapons, name and toes must not be cropped.
- Fine lines, frames and module spacing form a shared grid. Patina, shadow, cloth, architecture or light may partly blend them into the visual world. Empty space is welcome; modules must not be compressed into illegibility.

## M2 – Individual character worlds (world series)

- M2 individualises models more strongly through their own worlds. Setting, architecture, props, light, materials and personal symbolism become as binding as the figure's appearance.
- The basic format may be **2:3 or square**. A square sheet is particularly suitable when documenting an additional outfit or equipment variant on the right.
- Scenic world area and factual reference modules remain clearly readable but form one shared setting rather than two separately designed boards.
- Larger, more detailed final files are expressly welcome. Reliable facial reference, full-body views, reproducible equipment and readable material and colour details are decisive.
- **Every M2 model includes a character-specific alternative outfit.** The main outfit shows the familiar core role; the alternative deliberately reveals another side documented in the profile, such as everyday life rather than ceremony, care rather than combat, craftsmanship rather than leadership or travel rather than court life.
- The alternative outfit is a narrative counterpoint, not merely a colour change. Cut, materials, equipment and posture must explain which other character side is being shown.
- Both outfits clearly show the same person. Face, anatomy, age, skin, hair, eyes, horns, scales and other unmistakable features remain identical. Recurring colours, materials or symbols connect the outfits without making them uniform.
- Document the alternative equipment with at least one clearly readable full-body view and suitable detail or portrait views on the same M2 sheet.
- The binding main version is named `Spielername_Modell_Weltserie.png`. Additional versions receive a descriptive suffix.

## Binding page grid

```text
┌──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│ NAME                 │ PORTRAIT  │ PROFILE     │
│ ROLE                 ├───────────┴─────────────┤
│ Traits               │ VIEWS                   │
│                      │ front │ side │ back     │
│                      ├─────────────────────────┤
│ dominant             │ DETAILS                 │
│ full-body figure     │ 4–5 distinctive         │
│ in hero pose         │ crops                   │
│                      ├─────────────────────────┤
│ Banner/emblem        │ EXPRESSIONS (SELECTION) │
│                      │ 4–5 facial expressions  │
│                      ├─────────────────────────┤
│ short motto          │ COLOUR PALETTE │ Profile│
└──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

The diagram describes information hierarchy, not a technical wireframe's visible form. The right-hand module order is binding. Only when a physical feature matters more than expressions may `DETAILS` and `EXPRESSIONS (SELECTION)` swap heights. Portrait, profile, views and colour palette always remain present.

## Shared design language

All character models combine **reference sheet and cinematic scene**. Both must credibly belong in the same visual space.

- The left column presents an atmospheric character scene. The right adopts its light, colours, materials and background depth in a calmer form.
- Dark surfaces show material character: blackened metal, weathered stone, aged leather, dark cloth, smoke, soot or highly restrained parchment texture. Avoid pure flawless flat black.
- Frames resemble thin aged brass or metal. Slight irregularities, wear, interrupted lines and local shadows are welcome.
- Modules may differ in height and visually follow the figure, banner or architecture. Perfect symmetry is not a goal.
- Background, banner, cloak or lighting may reach module boundaries or overlap them locally, provided labels and references stay clear.
- Composition has a narrative hierarchy: face and hero figure first, then name and silhouette, followed by portrait and views, and finally details, expressions and palette.
- Every sheet gains character-specific identity through name lettering, accent colour, setting and a confirmed personal motif. The shared grid must not flatten individuality.
- The overall impression is high-quality and developed but not sterile. Patina, wear, slight atmospheric irregularity and spatial depth belong to DlE style.

## Area allocation

### Left column: identity and impact

- **Header, approximately 9–13 % of page height:** player name at the largest typographic level; role or archetype below in considerably smaller capitals.
- **Trait list, approximately 10–18 %:** four to at most eight short terms in a vertical list with small diamond, star, crown or dragon marks. They describe personality or function and do not replace visual references. Large round symbol buttons and uniform icon tiles are not permitted.
- **Hero portrayal, approximately 65–78 %:** show the figure as fully as possible from head to feet. The pose conveys personality but stays neutral enough to assess clothing and silhouette.
- **Motto, approximately 5–9 % at the bottom:** one short confirmed sentence of no more than two lines. It may be omitted to show the figure larger and more clearly.
- Alliance banner, dragon emblem or location reference sit behind or beside the figure and remain visually subordinate.

### Right column: reproducible reference

1. **Portrait and profile, approximately 20–24 %:** two equally sized head images. Frontal or slight three-quarter view on the left, true profile on the right. Head size, light and crop are comparable.
2. **Views, approximately 27–34 %:** at least front, side and back; a second side or three-quarter view is optional. Neutral posture, same scale, consistent clothing and calm dark background.
3. **Details, approximately 12–17 %:** four to five similarly framed crops. Show only identity-defining features, such as eyes, jewellery, tattoo, weapon, armour, cloak emblem or companion.
4. **Expressions (selection), approximately 10–14 %:** four to five tightly cropped facial images with clearly distinct but credible expressions.
5. **Colour palette and short profile, approximately 6–10 %:** five to seven circular swatches ordered main colour, secondary colour, metal, skin/fur, accent and optionally magic or eye colour. Beside them, no more than four brief character terms.

Right-column modules share a baseline but need not resemble identical cards. Varying heights, individual open edges and partial integration into background or light give the series its organic character.

## Visual language

- High-quality, cinematic, semi-realistic dark-fantasy illustration resembling refined game art.
- Credible anatomy, materials and lighting; no distinctly cartoonish, anime-like or arbitrarily photographic portrayal.
- Dark navy, charcoal and black form the shared base. Aged gold, brass and warm amber light connect all character sheets with the DlE visual world.
- Individual accent colours may vary strongly, for example red, green or blue. Use them selectively in clothing, emblem, rim light and palette, rather than a full-image colour cast.
- Backgrounds show castle, alliance hall, city or camp atmospherically. They support origin and role without outshining figure or reference modules.
- The background does not stop abruptly at the central axis. Light, architecture, banners, smoke or floor texture subtly connect both columns.
- The main character is the brightest, highest-contrast information on the left. Reference views on the right are more factual and evenly lit where possible.
- Metals, leather, cloth, skin, hair and fur retain the same materials and colours in every view.
- Surfaces show fine wear and credible irregularities. Smooth gloss, flawless vector edges and a premium-product advertising look do not suit the series.

## Character consistency

Before designing, record unchanging features in writing:

- face shape, apparent age and skin or fur colour
- eye colour and distinctive eye features
- hairstyle, hair colour, beard, horns or ears
- height, build and proportions
- scars, tattoos, scales or other skin features
- main clothing, armour silhouette and cloak shape
- recurring colours and metal type
- jewellery, weapon, amulet, bag or companion
- position and shape of dragon or personal emblem

These features agree across hero pose, portrait, profile, views, details and expressions. An accessory may be absent only when the crop naturally excludes it. Figure variants belong on their own clearly labelled model sheet and must not be mixed without explanation.

## Typography and labels

- The player name is the only strongly individualised typographic element. A classical, Gothic, engraved or rough handwritten display font should suit the figure while remaining instantly readable. Lettering can be distinctive but must not dominate the header like an oversized logo.
- Role and module headings use a calm medieval serif in capitals.
- Body text and trait lists stay plain, high-contrast and substantially smaller than the name.
- Colours: warm ivory or antique gold; individual accent colour only for small symbols, dividing lines or selected initials.
- Binding German module names: `PORTRÄT`, `PROFIL`, `ANSICHTEN`, `VORNE`, `SEITE`, `HINTEN`, `DETAILS`, `MIMIK (AUSWAHL)` and `FARBPALETTE`.
- Check text after image generation and cleanly reset it if necessary. Illegible AI lettering, pseudo-words and random symbols must never remain on a final model sheet.
- Write the alliance name exclusively as **DlE**. `DRACHENRITTER` may appear as a role label when expressly requested in the content; it does not replace the current alliance name.

## Frames, ornaments and branding

- Fine frames in aged gold, brass or accent colour separate modules.
- Frames are thin and consistent in material, but may be visibly weathered, locally open or partly dissolved by shadow and background.
- A closed, flawlessly symmetrical outer frame is unnecessary. Architecture, darkness and floor may finish the edge more organically.
- Avoid heavy decorative frames, strongly glowing outlines and consistently clean vector aesthetics.
- A dragon emblem may appear on banner, cloak, armour or seal. Its shape and position must remain identical throughout the sheet.
- At most one large emblem on the left and one small branding element in the footer.
- Crowns, hearts, runes or regional references are allowed only when they belong to the confirmed character.
- Signatures are optional and must not cover palette or reference data.

## Template for new characters

Complete at least this briefing before generating images:

```text
PLAYER NAME:
ROLE / ARCHETYPE:
4–8 CHARACTER TRAITS:
FACE / APPARENT AGE:
SKIN / EYES / HAIR:
BUILD:
CLOTHING / ARMOUR:
MAIN COLOURS:
ACCENT COLOUR:
METAL TYPE:
4–5 REQUIRED DETAILS:
WEAPON / ACCESSORY / COMPANION:
EMBLEM:
SETTING:
OPTIONAL MOTTO:
```

Do not replace missing information with random complex tattoos, crests, weapons or jewellery. For open details, prefer a simple solution that can later be reproduced.

## Recommended production order

1. Define the character briefing and unchanging features.
2. Generate a clean neutral full-body view plus portrait and profile.
3. Approve face, build, clothing and details before creating further variants.
4. Derive front, side and back views from the same approved model.
5. Add detail crops and expressions with identical features.
6. Only then assemble hero pose, background, text, frames, emblem and palette into the final sheet.
7. Check the completed sheet at original and reduced size.

## Quality control

Store a new character model only when every question can be answered yes:

1. Does M1 use 2:3, or M2 a justified 2:3 or square format with clearly ordered modules?
2. Is the figure in hero pose and views the same person with identical build?
3. Do face, skin, eyes, hair, scars, tattoos, horns and ears agree in every field?
4. Are clothing, armour, emblem, jewellery and weapons structurally consistent?
5. Do front, side and back actually show the same clothing without invented elements?
6. Are portrait and profile clearly recognisable rather than unusably obscured by hood, hair or shadows?
7. Do detail fields help reproduce the figure later?
8. Do expressions visibly differ without changing face or apparent age?
9. Is all text correct, readable and free of pseudo-words?
10. Is the sheet free of extra limbs, fused accessories or illogical weapons?
11. Is the background calmer than figure and reference area?
12. Does the reduced sheet still work as a clearly organised reference?
13. Does the sheet resemble a cinematic fantasy concept board rather than a modern user interface?
14. Do frames, surfaces and materials have credible patina and spatial depth?
15. Are hero scene and reference modules visibly connected through light, colour or setting?
16. Is the trait list restrained and free of large icon buttons?
17. Does the M2 alternative outfit show another documented side rather than merely a recoloured variant?
18. Does the figure clearly remain the same person in both outfits through every identity feature?

## Avoid

- square or free formats without a functional reason; M2 expressly permits square format for additional world or outfit modules
- a pure hero scene without factual views
- changing faces, hairstyles, body shapes or ages within a sheet
- differing clothing between hero pose, views and details
- full-body views that are too small or cropped feet, horns and weapons
- more than eight trait words or long explanatory blocks
- purely decorative detail fields without recognisability value
- sterile card grids, perfect mirror symmetry and a modern dashboard appearance
- large round trait buttons or uniform icon tiles
- flawlessly glossy gold frames, clean vector lines and glossy advertising aesthetics
- a hard design division between hero scene and reference column
- harsh science-fiction interfaces and neon frames
- random crests, signatures, slogans and unconfirmed character traits
- blurred, low-resolution or visibly compressed final files

## Storage and naming

Store final reference models under `Galerie/Charaktermodelle`.

```text
Spielername_Referenzmodell.Dateiendung
Spielername_Modell_Weltserie.Dateiendung
```

Further views and character-specific scenes follow the naming rules in `Galerie/Charaktermodelle/README.md`. PNG is recommended as the final format; JPEG makes sense only for deliberately smaller files without transparency or fine text artefacts.
