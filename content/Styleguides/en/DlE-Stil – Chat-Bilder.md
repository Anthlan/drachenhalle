# DlE style – Chat and reaction images / snapshots

**Goal:**  
A shared visual language for scenic chat images and short, reusable reaction images. Purpose and rendering style are treated independently.

## Purpose

- **Chat image:** scenic or narrative subject, recurring joke, special moment or small alliance story
- **Reaction image:** short reusable reply or greeting such as “Looks good”, “Great”, “Excuse me?”, “Morning” or “Good night”

The category follows purpose rather than style. A chat image may therefore use S3; a reaction image is not automatically S3.

## Binding standard style

S1 chat images use the mature DlE alliance style: high-quality, cinematic, semi-realistic fantasy illustration with stable character models, dark-blue-and-gold design and warm amber light. S2 and S3 carry the same recognisability into more explanatory or deliberately exaggerated renderings. S4 provides a deliberately separate post-apocalyptic poster world alongside them.

Chibi subjects also follow the separate `DlE-Stil – Chibi-Chatbilder.md` guideline, whether used as chat or reaction images.

## Style groups

Four broad style groups describe the rendering. S1 to S3 belong to the blue-and-gold DlE visual world; S4 is a deliberately distinct “Z Routes Redemption” series with a different poster aesthetic:

### S1 – Cinematic alliance fantasy

The standard for coherent scenes. Figures, setting and action form a credible cinematic snapshot. Short speech bubbles, signs or organically embedded text are possible, provided the scene defines the image.

### S2 – Fantasy comic & infographic

For subjects needing more room for explanations, recurring jokes or game mechanics. Multiple panels, clear speech bubbles, checklists, UI elements and bold headings are allowed. Character models, colours, materials and light remain tied to normal DlE style; avoid sterile standard infographics and raw screenshots without design integration.

### S3 – Chibi fantasy

For strongly simplified, cute and deliberately exaggerated portrayals with clear silhouettes and simplified proportions. S3 suits both short reactions and scenic chibi chat images.

### S4 – Z Routes post-apocalypse

For bold survival and character posters in a humorous post-apocalyptic world. Features include grainy materials, strong black-red-white contrast, warm sunsets, rough brush or stencil lettering, handwritten margin notes and visible “Z Routes Redemption” branding. Figures may be more caricatured than in S1 or S2, but must remain recognisable from reference models and recurring attributes. S4 suits individual posters, recurring jokes, group scenes and alliance advertising that looks like part of the same rugged campaign series.

The style group is documented centrally in `media/gallery/STILINDEX.md`, rather than encoded in filenames or folder structure. Introduce new groups only when an image cannot reasonably fit an existing one.

### Appearance
- High-quality **cinematic, semi-realistic fantasy illustration** resembling refined game art
- Cinematic composition with spatial depth, credible materials and clear lighting
- A lightly stylised or illustrative touch is allowed, but must not become distinctly cartoonish or shift between anime styles
- Warm light from candles, hearths, lanterns or sunlight; prefer golden and amber accents
- Dominant colours:
  - dark navy as base colour
  - gold as defining accent
  - warm amber, brown and orange for lighting and setting
  - black only to support depth and contrast
- Recurring settings:
  - grand alliance hall
  - strategy room
  - castle interior
  - map or meeting table

### Figures
- 1–4 clearly recognisable main figures
- Existing character models are binding visual references
- Consistently retain face, hairstyle, build, clothing, colours and signature accessories
- Do not spontaneously redesign a known figure to fit a single scene
- Attractive, stylised and distinctive
- Expression and body language should tell the scene
- Figures may react with deliberate slight exaggeration when it supports humour
- Supporting figures remain visually subordinate and must not displace the main figures

### Scenic elements
- Recurring props such as strategy maps, books, cups, scrolls, tablets, buttons, miniatures or medals strengthen the shared visual world
- Text may be organically embedded on:
  - banners
  - boards
  - books
  - notes
  - cups
  - speech bubbles
- Keep text short, readable and preferably a natural part of the scene; handwritten-looking lettering is possible
- Small Easter eggs and inside jokes are expressly welcome
- Every portrayal should tell a concrete moment or small action and look like a real slice of the same DlE world

### Tone & effect
- Charming
- slightly self-deprecating
- friendly
- strategic
- occasionally absurd or playful
- Still high-quality rather than cartoonish

### Core feeling
**“A strong fantasy alliance that does not take itself too seriously.”**

### Avoid
- Pure infographic
- Generic fantasy portraits without action
- Changing drawing styles or contradictory character portrayals within a series
- Extremely photorealistic, strongly cartoonish or distinctly anime-like stylistic breaks
- Overly dark horror or zombie appearance
- Oversexualised portrayal
- Too much text
- Figures without recognisable interaction
