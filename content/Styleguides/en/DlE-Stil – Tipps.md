# DlE style – Tips

## Goal

Tips are standalone knowledge graphics for Z:Route: Redemption, ready to share directly. They combine the cinematic atmosphere of DlE chat images with the information depth of a compact game guide.

The graphic should communicate the complete accompanying tip text understandably. An uncluttered design means clear organisation, rather than omitting gameplay-relevant details.

## Audience and perspective

- Tips primarily address our own alliance members.
- Write articles, graphic text and copyable announcements from our shared perspective: preferably with **we**, **us** and **our**, addressing members directly with **you** and **you all**.
- Explain how we act as an alliance and what you can do concretely. Do not write as outside observers about “the alliance” or “its members” where “we” or “our members” fits naturally.
- Game mechanics, feature names and general facts may be described neutrally. Frame recommendations, processes and assessments as our shared knowledge.
- The complete overarching rules are in [`DlE – Text formatting`](DlE%20%E2%80%93%20Textformatierung.md).

## Principle

**Complete information takes priority over decorative reduction.**

- Every relevant game mechanic in the approved text also appears in the graphic.
- Include examples, numbers, sequences, conditions and warnings in full.
- Symbols support text but do not replace necessary explanation.
- Wording may be shortened if meaning and limitations remain complete.
- Do not add unconfirmed mechanics or present them as fact.

## Format and structure

- Portrait format with a **2:3** ratio, preferably 1024 × 1536 pixels or larger at the same ratio.
- Fixed visual order:
  1. Series header `TIPS & TRICKS`
  2. Tip number and unambiguous title
  3. short key statement or instruction
  4. detailed main section with process, comparison or example
  5. clear benefit or concrete recommendation
  6. warning, limitation or important addition
- The main section receives the largest area. Title and decoration must not displace explanatory content.
- Depending on the topic, two to four clearly separated information blocks are permitted.
- Related content shares calm surfaces; different cases appear in columns or consecutive steps.
- The graphic resembles a page from a high-quality strategy book in the alliance hall rather than a brightly coloured event poster.

## Information density

- A tip graphic may deliberately contain more text than a general alliance announcement.
- Short paragraphs of two to four lines are allowed and welcome when explaining a mechanic.
- Process examples use numbered steps, arrows or pairs.
- If an existing reference graphic contains a detailed calculation, sequence or combat scheme, include it as an understandable example rather than reducing it to the basic rule.
- Clearly label illustrative outcomes as **examples**. Distinguish the general rule from an example process.
- Highlight specific values and outcomes at a sufficient size without removing explanatory context.
- Remove repetition and pure advertising phrases in favour of gameplay-relevant information.
- Empty space serves readability rather than being an end in itself.

## Visual language

- High-quality, cinematic, semi-realistic fantasy illustration in the atmosphere of normal DlE chat images.
- Overall darker, calmer and substantially less saturated than alliance announcements or event posters.
- Base colours: deep navy, charcoal, dark wood and blackened metal.
- Accents: aged gold and brass rather than bright yellow gold; warm subdued amber light rather than harsh illumination.
- Green appears only as restrained moss or olive for correct processes and benefits.
- Red appears only as muted brick or wine red for wrong approaches and warnings.
- Background: atmospheric strategy room, dark map table, open tome, parchments, candles and blurred castle architecture.
- Text surfaces preferably use dark slightly transparent material, aged parchment or inset boards. Avoid large fully glowing colour boxes.
- Spatial depth, credible materials, subtle wear and soft lighting visibly connect tips with the chat-image world.
- Figures are at most blurred secondary subjects, never the main subject.

## In-game screenshots and direct usability

When a tip explains a specific action, button, view or menu sequence, **original in-game screenshots take priority over newly illustrated recreations**.

- Retain existing in-game crops as faithfully as possible so players immediately recognise the interface, button and result.
- Cropping, scaling, framing and integration with tip styling are allowed. Do not alter or stylise the actual interface so heavily that readers must mentally translate it into the real game view.
- Highlight important controls with restrained arrows, circles, numbers or short labels.
- Where possible, multistep procedures show the matching original views in their actual order.
- Illustrations and relief symbols supplement in-game crops rather than replacing them where interface recognition matters for carrying out the action.
- Use a free recreation only if no suitable screenshot exists or the available crop is unreadable.
- Check in-game crops for currency, readability and agreement with the approved tip.

## Symbols

- Explanatory symbols take centre stage: clear line-art or relief symbols for base, target, resource tile, troops, shield, clock, eye, healing and sequence.
- Symbols resemble embossed brass signs, ink drawings, map markers or small game pieces on a strategy table.
- Arrows, numbers, connecting lines and state changes explain processes clearly.
- Prefer simple symbol sequences to elaborate miniature scenes, building renderings or battle paintings.
- Give each symbol a short label; meaning must not depend on colour alone.
- Small detail notes, margin notes and legends are welcome when they explain mechanics more precisely.
- Decorative symbols stay restrained and must not compete with explanatory symbols.

## Typography

- Distinctive but not oversized serif type in antique gold or warm ivory for tip number and main title.
- Clear high-contrast sans-serif type in warm ivory or pale parchment for explanations, values and labels.
- Text must stay readable in a reduced chat view.
- Keep headings short; explanatory sentences have their own surfaces.
- Check terms, numbers and punctuation exactly. Retain German umlauts.

## Series features

Every tip graphic repeatedly uses:

- the header `TIPS & TRICKS`,
- the form `TIP #N`,
- a subtle dragon as embossed seal, watermark or small emblem,
- fine frames and restrained lily or dragon ornaments of aged brass,
- a recommendation with a subdued green accent,
- a notice area with subdued red accent where a warning or limitation exists.

Write the alliance name exclusively as **DlE**. If a generated graphic cannot reliably render it correctly, use a neutral dragon emblem without letters instead.

## Quality review

Check every tip graphic before storing it:

1. Are all statements from the approved text represented in substance?
2. Are examples, numbers, sequences and conditions correct?
3. Are existing multistep examples shown fully rather than merely summarised?
4. Is the main action immediately recognisable?
5. Are warnings and limitations complete?
6. Does explanatory text remain readable at small sizes?
7. Is the graphic free of invented mechanics or additional slogans?
8. Have historical names such as `DRR` and `Drachenritter` been removed?
9. Are existing in-game screenshots integrated as faithfully as possible for concrete interface actions?

## Avoid

- Excessive simplification that loses information
- Pure symbol graphics without sufficient explanation
- Very large titles at the expense of main content
- Small tightly packed walls of text without visual organisation
- Decorative slogans without gameplay value
- Highly saturated colours, glowing gold and large traffic-light-coloured areas
- Elaborate building, troop or battle scenes where simple symbols explain the process more clearly
- The appearance of an event poster, modern dashboard interface or glossy advertisement
- Modern science-fiction interfaces or apocalyptic black-and-red styling
- Historical alliance identifiers in current graphics
- Invented or strongly stylised recreations of an existing in-game interface when an original screenshot is available
