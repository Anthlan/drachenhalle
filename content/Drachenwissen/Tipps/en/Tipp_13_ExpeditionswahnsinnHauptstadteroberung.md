# 💡 Tip #13 – Expedition Frenzy: Capital Conquest

## Key points

- From Sunday through Friday, jointly collected **Warzone Points** determine which server attacks on Saturday and which defends.
- During the battle phase, the shared server objective matters: attackers must capture the opposing capital, while defenders must prevent this.
- The four **batteries** surrounding the capital are not secondary targets. Controlled and well-garrisoned batteries put enemy troops inside the capital under pressure.
- An alliance achieves **maximum impact** with fixed raid teams: a responsible rally leader, four assigned participants, prepared targets, signals and substitutes.
- Less coordinated rallies can also be strong and successful. They usually take longer, react more slowly and achieve optimal results less reliably.
- If a small alliance cannot field viable raid teams or hold an objective, **Hit & Run** is a deliberate alternative: attack briefly and relocate in time before a superior counterattack.
- No uncoordinated solo attacks against the capital. After rotating, damaged teams heal, reorganize and deploy again only on instruction.
- Offensive actions such as attacking, scouting or joining an attack can end a shield and trigger **Battle Frenzy**. Check the current notice before every action.
- According to the current DIE calendar, the battle phase lasts at most four hours and may end earlier once one side reaches **100% progress**.

> **Remember:** Use fixed raid teams for maximum objective pressure; when outnumbered, deliberately switch to controlled Hit & Run.

## What is this tip for?

This tip explains Capital Conquest within **Expedition Frenzy**. The event is cross-server combat: several alliances from the same server jointly pursue an objective against their assigned opposing server.

This page aims to prevent three common problems:

1. Players confuse the weekly scoring phase with the actual battle phase.
2. Everyone focuses on the capital and leaves batteries empty.
3. Members attack individually, lose their shields and consume troops without supporting the shared attack.
4. Small alliances try defending an untenable position when a mobile Hit & Run plan would preserve troops better.

Capital Conquest described here is not equivalent to an ordinary capital capture within your own server. Rules, permitted targets and cooperation may differ.

## Expedition Frenzy in three phases

| Phase | Period | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warzone scoring | Sunday through Friday | The server jointly collects Warzone Points. |
| Evaluation | Friday | The higher-scoring server becomes attacker; the other becomes defender. |
| Capital Conquest | Saturday | Both servers fight over the capital, batteries and progress bar. |

Warzone Points and personal points serve different purposes:

- **Warzone Points** determine attacking and defending roles during the week.
- **Personal points** are earned during the battle phase and matter for personal rewards.
- **Server victory** depends on the shared conquest or defense objective.

Anyone pursuing only personal scoring may be missing from the server objective. Before battle, decide whether a player is assigned to the capital, a battery, support or another approved task.

## Main plan and alternative tactic

Capital Conquest has a clear tactical order of priority. Objective fighting with fixed raid teams is the best application of coordinated rallies and has the greatest influence on the capital and batteries. However, not every alliance has enough strong, consistently available players to implement this fully.

| Approach | Goal | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main plan – fixed raid teams** | capture, occupy and rotate through the capital and batteries with prepared five-person groups | optimal organization for maximum impact; requires clear responsibility and enough active participants |
| **Uncoordinated rally** | form strong individual rallies with currently available members | still useful, but slower, more error-prone and less reliable than a fixed raid team |
| **Alternative – Hit & Run** | make brief attacks, avoid superior enemy forces and preserve troops | sensible when raid teams are unavailable or the objective cannot be held permanently |

Hit & Run therefore does not equal optimally organized objective fighting; it is a deliberate alternative when outnumbered or insufficiently active. It is nevertheless not a disorganized retreat: targets, stop signals and escape routes are agreed beforehand.

### Hit & Run as an alternative for small alliances

When holding permanently is unrealistic, attack only clearly chosen targets and remain mobile. Once a superior counterattack becomes apparent, two prepared withdrawal routes are available:

1. **Raid jump:** Since Capital Conquest may coincide with Raid in Alliance Competition, the event can be used to jump to the opposing server when threatened. According to current DIE experience, troops outside the base do not need recalling first.
2. **Random Relocator:** If Raid jumping is unavailable, recall all troops to the base. Then use a Random Relocator – strictly before the battle is already underway.

The basics are in Tip #10 – Hit & Run in PvP. Tip #11 – Fixed raid teams in PvP explains preparing and leading fixed five-person groups. Tip #12 – Read battle reports correctly helps with the subsequent review.

## Read the schedule correctly

In-game times refer to server time. For current September and October 2026 dates, the DIE calendar lists the battle phase, for example, from **16:00 to 20:00 German local time**. Do not permanently assume this local time: daylight-saving changes and game updates may alter the conversion.

Before every event, therefore check:

1. the date and time in the DIE calendar,
2. attacker or defender status after Friday’s evaluation,
3. when access to the opposing server opens,
4. the actual battle-phase start, and
5. current victory conditions in the event window.

Current documentation states that the battle phase ends after at most four hours or earlier when either side reaches **100% progress**. The current in-game text takes priority for the specific event.

## Capital and batteries

The capital is at the center. Four batteries surround it:

| Map position | Name |
| --- | --- |
| upper left | West |
| upper right | North |
| lower left | South |
| lower right | East |

According to the publicly documented mechanic, a battery controlled by your server fires on enemy teams inside the capital. The battery does not capture the capital itself, but makes it harder for the opponent to hold it permanently.

This creates a simple priority:

> **The capital decides the outcome – the batteries make holding it sustainable or unsustainable.**

An attack focusing only on the capital can therefore fail even after a successful first rally. If enemy batteries remain active, the new garrisons continue weakening. Conversely, a full battery offers little benefit if nobody captures or defends the capital.

### Community-documented battery values

The detailed community guide currently documents the following values. They help with planning, but the DIE archive has not independently confirmed them through its own current screenshots:

| Garrisoned teams | Documented reload time |
| ---: | ---: |
| 0–4 | 10 seconds |
| 5–9 | 9 seconds |
| 10–14 | 8 seconds |
| 15–19 | 7 seconds |
| 20 | 6 seconds |

Accordingly, every fifth team reduces reload time by one second; at 20 teams, the documented minimum would be reached. Check the current capacity and effect descriptions in-game before deployment. Regardless of individual numbers, this remains sound for practical alliance planning: **A captured but barely occupied battery exerts less pressure than a fully supported one.**

## Assign roles for the objective plan

These names are not official game roles. They describe tasks to assign before starting.

### Operational leadership

Operational leadership watches the map and progress, places markers and authorizes:

- which target to attack first,
- who starts a rally,
- when to reinforce the capital or a battery,
- when damaged teams rotate, and
- when to abandon a target to secure a more important one.

During an ongoing attack, only a small number of clearly identified players should give orders.

### Capital group

This group contains the strongest reliably combat-ready teams. Their task is not to continually march solo into the capital, but to:

1. remove enemy garrisons through coordinated rallies,
2. quickly deploy friendly teams after a successful capture,
3. relieve damaged garrisons in time, and
4. immediately answer an enemy takeover with a prepared counterattack.

For maximum impact, the capital group works in fixed raid teams with one responsible rally leader and four assigned participants. Name a substitute beforehand where possible. If this structure is not fully possible, the alliance can still form strong rallies with available members; allow more time for calls, team checks and replacing participants.

### Battery groups

Assign at least one responsible player or fixed group to each prioritized battery. Battery groups:

- capture their assigned target,
- report strong enemy garrisons,
- fill available slots,
- rotate damaged teams, and
- do not leave their position for a distant solo target.

Two reliably controlled batteries can be a sensible intermediate goal; four controlled batteries create maximum shared pressure. Operational leadership decides what is realistic in the specific battle based on participation and enemy strength.

### Support and position security

Weaker players can also contribute decisively:

- completely fill approved rallies,
- occupy batteries with still-usable teams,
- reinforce bases within their own alliance,
- block landing spots and approaches near important players,
- report enemy markers and movements, and
- provide alliance help for healing between attack waves.

Cross-alliance cooperation on the same server is possible at the shared objective. However, according to current community documentation, members still cannot join another alliance’s rally or reinforce another alliance’s base. Plan rallies and base protection within your own alliance.

## Preparation before battle

### On Friday

- Check attacker or defender status.
- Evaluate objective fighting with fixed raid teams as the main plan; choose Hit & Run as the alternative if strength or activity is insufficient.
- Name operational leadership, rally leaders, four fixed participants and substitutes for each raid team.
- Assign capital and battery groups.
- Ask about participation and approximate online periods.
- Agree markers, chat channel and short commands.
- Explain which personal actions members should subordinate to the server objective.

### Before access opens

- Check main team, troop strength and march slots.
- Empty the hospital and check capacity.
- Prepare healing, march and relocation resources.
- Check active combat bonuses and their duration.
- Activate a peace shield only with the knowledge that offensive actions can end it.
- Reopen Tip #8 on rapid healing and organize enough helpers.

### Before battle starts

- Take the assigned position.
- Do not scout or attack without approval.
- Check which player opens which rally.
- Name battery and capital symbols clearly to avoid confusing North, South, East and West.
- Assign the first replacement teams and rotation order.

## Attack plan for the attacking server

### 1. Secure positions

Attackers should use the available access and positioning phase. Short marches and free landing spots beside strong players become valuable during battle.

### 2. Choose a shared opening target

Before starting, operational leadership decides whether to attack a specific battery, several batteries or the capital directly. The plan must suit actual strength and participant count.

### 3. Break garrisons with rallies

Solo attacks on a strong capital garrison spread wounded soldiers, reveal positions and may trigger Battle Frenzy. The rally leader starts, the four assigned participants immediately fill with approved teams, and everyone else waits for the result. If a member is unavailable, the pre-named substitute steps in. A strong rally remains possible without fixed raid teams, but requires a longer gathering and checking phase.

### 4. Occupy immediately after capture

A successful attack is only the transition point. Fresh teams must enter the target immediately while damaged teams return and heal.

### 5. Stabilize batteries in parallel

While the capital group works, battery groups reduce enemy bombardment and increase friendly pressure. Weaker teams may be tactically more valuable here than in an uncoordinated attack against the capital.

## Defense plan for the defending server

### 1. Occupy good positions early

Defenders know the target area. Strong players and assigned supporters should occupy important spots before the start, rather than arrive after the first attack.

### 2. Do not view the capital in isolation

A strong capital garrison without battery control faces continuous bombardment. Battery defense is therefore part of capital defense.

### 3. Keep replacement garrisons ready

After an enemy rally, it must immediately be clear who replaces eliminated or damaged teams. Do not begin searching for fresh teams only once the capital is empty.

### 4. Coordinate counterattacks

When the opponent captures a target, its involved teams are often already damaged. A prepared counterattack uses this brief window better than several delayed solo attacks.

## Shield and Battle Frenzy

According to current community documentation, these basic rules apply:

| Action | Documented shield consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Attack capital, battery or base | shield ends; Battle Frenzy |
| Join an offensive rally | shield ends; Battle Frenzy |
| Scout a target | shield ends; Battle Frenzy |
| Hold a position passively | shield remains possible |
| Reinforce a member’s base within your own alliance | shield remains possible |

Battle Frenzy is listed there as **15 minutes**, preventing a new shield during that time. DIE event notices also specify 15 minutes. Nevertheless, check the current warning before every action, since updates or special rules may alter individual cases.

> **Safety rule:** Anyone assigned to support under a shield must not attack “just briefly” or scout independently.

## Rotate, heal and redeploy

A team is not automatically fresh after winning. Its remaining troop count determines whether it can continue holding.

A clean cycle looks like this:

`fight → occupy target → watch damage → relieve → return → heal in small batches → redeploy`

Three rules help:

1. **Do not wait for complete collapse.** Timely rotation preserves readiness.
2. **Organize relief before withdrawal.** The damaged team leaves the garrison only once a replacement is marching or has arrived.
3. **Accelerate healing together.** Small batches and active alliance help return teams faster; see Tip #8.

## Deliberately plan server objectives and personal points

Capital Conquest offers personal rankings, but not every high-scoring action contributes equally to victory. In the optimal objective plan, an assigned player pursuing a distant solo target is missing from the decisive rally. Conversely, mobile individual targets may be intended under an explicitly ordered Hit & Run alternative.

Before battle, therefore establish:

- which players exclusively support the server objective,
- which players follow the mobile Hit & Run plan,
- when personal points may be collected,
- which targets must explicitly not be attacked, and
- when all players must return to the capital.

Personal rewards are worthwhile. What matters is that the objective plan takes priority while the alliance fields viable raid teams for it. Assign a Hit & Run group separately and deliberately; it must not tie up committed rally participants.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing weekly scoring with battle:** Warzone Points determine attack and defense; they do not replace Saturday’s fighting.
- **Watching only the capital:** Without batteries, a newly captured garrison quickly comes under pressure.
- **Marching individually into strong garrisons:** This creates losses without securing a coordinated takeover.
- **Not replenishing after capture:** An empty or weakly occupied target is immediately lost again.
- **Exhausting every strong player simultaneously:** No fresh group remains for a counterattack.
- **Keeping damaged teams too long:** Timely rotation is better than complete elimination.
- **Accidentally losing a shield:** Scouting or rally participation can also count as offensive actions.
- **Misplanning other alliances:** A shared objective does not automatically allow support for other alliances’ rallies or bases.
- **Chasing personal points:** The server outcome is decided at the capital and batteries.
- **Forcing an untenable position:** Small alliances can deliberately choose Hit & Run rather than consume all troops at the objective.
- **Assuming fixed times forever:** Recheck server time, German local time and daylight-saving changes for every event.

## Compact deployment checklist

### Before starting

- [ ] Attack or defense confirmed
- [ ] fixed raid teams assigned to objectives, or Hit & Run deliberately chosen as the alternative
- [ ] current time and access phase checked
- [ ] operational leadership, rally leader, four participants and substitute named for each raid team
- [ ] capital and battery groups assigned
- [ ] hospital, troops, march slots and relocators prepared
- [ ] shield and Battle Frenzy rules read
- [ ] markers and chat commands agreed

### During battle

- [ ] prioritize the objective plan; use Hit & Run only when assigned accordingly
- [ ] fill rallies completely with suitable teams
- [ ] occupy the capital immediately after a successful attack
- [ ] do not leave batteries empty
- [ ] relieve damaged teams in time
- [ ] heal in small batches and provide alliance help
- [ ] report progress and enemy movements

## Limitations and open questions

Several detailed values still lack our own current DIE in-game evidence. These include particularly:

- the exact progress-bar calculation,
- battery reload times and current thresholds,
- damage and treatment of soldiers hit by batteries,
- complete personal point values,
- possible differences between expedition rounds, and
- changes from later updates.

The strategy therefore distinguishes stable principles – coordinated rallies, capital plus batteries, roles and rotation – from version-dependent numbers. If information conflicts, the current in-game description always takes priority.
