# 💡 Tip #2 – Rally attacks

## Key points

- **Put strong teams at the front:** The leading teams fight first and, if they win, can eliminate further opponents.
- **Only one defending team:** Attackers face the same increasingly weakened team in succession.
- **Several defending teams:** A1 first fights V1, A2 fights V2, and so on. The surviving teams are then paired again in their existing order.
- **Winners continue fighting while weakened:** Damage already sustained remains; wounded soldiers can only be healed after returning to your own base.

> **Remember:** Position determines the first opponent – the result determines the next round.

![Tip 2 – Battle order in rally attacks](../../../../Drachenwissen/Tipps/Tipp_02_Sammelangriffe.png)

## What is this tip for?

This tip explains the **battle order within a rally attack**. Understanding the order helps you decide:

- which team belongs in the first position,
- why not every team sent along necessarily fights,
- how several attackers gradually weaken a single defending team,
- how pairings between multiple defenders continue across several rounds, and
- why a poorly positioned weak team can make the entire rally unnecessarily costly.

This tip concerns the **order of complete player or march teams within the rally**. The arrangement of the five heroes inside an individual team is a separate layer and is not covered here.

## Terms and basic rule

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **A1, A2, A3 …** | Attacking team in position 1, 2, 3 … |
| **V1, V2, V3 …** | Defending team in position 1, 2, 3 … |
| **Round** | A sequence of direct pairings between the teams currently remaining |
| **Remaining team** | A team that won its encounter and can therefore continue fighting |

The first round begins with matching positions:

`A1 against V1 → A2 against V2 → A3 against V3 → A4 against V4 …`

After each encounter, the defeated team is eliminated. The winning team remains in battle in its current condition. If teams remain on both sides afterward, the survivors are paired again **in their existing order**. This repeats until one side has no teams left.

> **Remember:** Position determines the first opponent. The result determines who fights again in the next round.

## Case 1: The defender has only one team

If only one defending team is available, the attackers fight that same team in succession. When the defender wins an encounter, it stays to face the next attacker – but carries the losses and damage from previous battles.

### Example sequence

| Encounter | Example result | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 against V1 | V1 wins | A1 is eliminated; V1 continues fighting while weakened. |
| A2 against V1 | V1 wins again | A2 is eliminated; V1 is weakened further. |
| A3 against V1 | A3 wins | V1 is eliminated; the defense ends. |
| A4 and later teams | No further battle | No defending team remains. |

This example shows two important points:

1. Attackers used early can weaken a strong defending team even when they lose.
2. As soon as the final defender is defeated, the battle ends. Attackers farther back in the order are not used.

## Case 2: Both sides have several teams

With several defending teams, the battle begins with pairings at matching positions. Only the winners then fight again.

### Round 1 – original order

For this illustration of the sequence only, assume the following results:

| Pairing | Example result | Remains in battle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 against V1 | A1 wins | A1 |
| A2 against V2 | V2 wins | V2 |
| A3 against V3 | A3 wins | A3 |
| A4 against V4 | V4 wins | V4 |

After round 1, the following remain:

- **Attackers:** A1, A3
- **Defenders:** V2, V4

### Round 2 – survivors are paired again

The remaining teams keep their relative order:

| Pairing | Example result | Remains in battle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 against V2 | A1 wins | A1 |
| A3 against V4 | V4 wins | V4 |

### Round 3 – the decision

Only A1 and V4 now remain:

`A1 against V4 → the winner decides the rally attack.`

These winners are merely an illustrative example to make the order clear. Actual in-game results depend on heroes, soldiers, research, equipment, bonuses, counters and damage already sustained, among other factors.

## Why strong teams belong at the front

A strong team in position 1 can do more than win its first pairing. If it remains combat-ready, it can eliminate further enemy teams in later rounds. This means:

- dangerous defenders are removed early,
- fewer subsequent attackers have to fight,
- the rally’s total losses often decrease, and
- more teams remain entirely or largely undamaged.

“Strong” does not simply mean the highest displayed combat power. Other decisive factors include:

- current troop strength and existing losses,
- hero roles and positions within the team,
- a suitable formation and faction bonus,
- research, equipment and active combat bonuses, and
- potential strengths or weaknesses against the opposing team.

> **Default rule:** If you have no detailed information about the defense, put the strongest reliably combat-ready team first. A deliberate counter formation can sometimes be more useful than simply choosing the highest combat power.

## What the alliance should coordinate before starting

1. **Define the target and purpose:** Is the goal a reliable victory, weakening a strong defender, or minimizing losses?
2. **Identify the strongest available team:** Combat power alone is not enough; troop numbers and formation must also be suitable.
3. **Check the displayed order:** Before starting, check which team actually occupies A1, A2, A3, and so on.
4. **Avoid unsuitable teams:** A weak or heavily damaged team should not block an important position near the front.
5. **Review the report after the battle:** Check the actual pairings, winners and remaining troops. This is the only way to determine whether the assumed order still applies and which formation needs improvement.

Check how positions can actually be assigned or changed in the current interface directly in the rally window before starting. Do not rely solely on the order in which players announced their participation in alliance chat.

## Common misconceptions

### “Every team sent along definitely fights”

No. Once the opposing side is completely defeated, the battle ends. Subsequent teams can therefore remain without having an encounter of their own.

### “Only the first battle matters”

No. Winners can fight again in later rounds. A strong team placed early can therefore affect or eliminate several opponents.

### “The highest combat power automatically wins”

Not necessarily. Composition, troop numbers, equipment, research, bonuses and prior damage influence the result. Use battle reports rather than relying on a single power figure.

### “A team that lost was useless”

Not necessarily. An inferior team may already damage the enemy and weaken it for the next attacker. Nevertheless, deliberately sacrificing a team is not a general default recommendation: poor ordering can create unnecessary wounded soldiers and keep a strong opponent in battle longer.

## Wounded soldiers and follow-up attacks

Order affects both victory and how losses are distributed. Teams that fight early and repeatedly may accumulate more wounded soldiers. After the rally:

> **Caution:** Wounded soldiers can only be healed after returning to your own base.

Plan more than the first attack. Also consider the return march, hospital capacity and whether another rally is planned immediately afterward.

## Practical review after a rally attack

Record at least these points from the battle report:

- Which teams actually occupied A1, A2, A3 or V1, V2, V3?
- Which initial pairing was shown for each team?
- Which winners fought again in a further round?
- With how many troops remaining did they enter the next battle?
- Which teams were never used?
- Could another order have avoided losses or delivered an earlier victory?

A single report shows a sequence. Several reports following the same pairing rules turn it into reliable alliance knowledge.

## Limitations and open questions

- The exact pairing logic comes from in-game observations and the alliance’s practical knowledge; research did not locate an official public detailed description.
- The interface and sorting options may change with updates. Check the displayed positions immediately before starting.
- It still needs confirmation whether exactly the same order applies to all rally types, or whether individual events or targets use special rules.
- Combat statistics alone do not explain every result. If there are discrepancies, the current in-game battle report takes priority.
