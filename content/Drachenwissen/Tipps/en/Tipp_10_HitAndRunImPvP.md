# 💡 Tip #10 – Hit & Run in PvP

## Key points

- **Hit & Run** means briefly attacking a worthwhile target and relocating your base before the opponent can effectively retaliate.
- For small alliances, this often makes more sense than defending an untenable position against much stronger opponents.
- **Escape route A – server travel through Raid:** This emergency exit works in both directions. If fighting on the opposing server, use **Return** to go home when threatened. If fighting on your own server during Capital Conquest, use **Alliance Competition → Raid → Go** to reach the opposing server. According to current DIE player experience, troops outside your base do not need to be recalled first.
- **Escape route B – Random Relocator:** Recall all troops to your base and use the Random Relocator before the battle starts. The new edge position cannot be planned, but quickly separates you from the previous danger point.
- Do not wait for impact. Once a battle is underway, a normal relocator can no longer be used.
- After every relocation, immediately check position, incoming marches, shield status, Battle Frenzy and your troops’ return routes.

> **Remember:** Strike briefly, recognize danger early, disappear in time.

## What is this tip for?

This tip describes a mobile PvP tactic for situations where holding your position permanently would be too costly or hopeless. This particularly applies to:

- small alliances facing much stronger opponents,
- Raid in Alliance Competition,
- Capital Conquest during Expedition Frenzy,
- open PvP phases with many incoming attacks, and
- individual attacks on poorly protected enemy bases.

Hit & Run is not an invitation to indiscriminate solo attacks. Attack a target only when the possible return justifies the risk and a working escape route is prepared.

## The basic principle

```text
Select target
      ↓
attack briefly and in a controlled way
      ↓
watch the enemy response
      ↓
relocate in time
      ↓
recheck situation, troops and protection
```

The tactic exchanges permanent position-holding for mobility. Give the opponent as little time as possible to:

- organize a rally,
- block your return position,
- attack your base repeatedly, or
- force you into a prolonged exchange with heavy losses.

Retreat is not a failure here. It is a planned part of the attack.

## Before every attack: Decide the escape route

Attack only once these questions are answered:

1. Is Raid currently open?
2. Are you fighting on your own or the opposing server – and which transfer takes you out of danger?
3. Is a Random Relocator in your inventory?
4. Can all normal marches be recalled quickly?
5. Is Battle Frenzy or another teleport-blocking status already active?
6. How will you identify the enemy response – an incoming march, rally flag, scouting or a strong player teleporting nearby?
7. At which signal will you stop without attempting “one last attack”?

> **Practical rule:** Decide the escape route before attacking, not under time pressure afterward.

## Escape route A: Change servers through Raid

During Alliance Competition, the German interface may label this section **Raubzug** or **Überfall**. Server travel can serve as an escape route in either direction:

- **You are fighting on the opposing server:** After attacking, use **Return** when threatened and jump to your own server.
- **You are fighting on your own server:** This can happen especially during Capital Conquest. When threatened, use **Alliance Competition → Raid → Go** to jump to the opposing server.

According to current DIE player experience, this cross-server transfer works even with your troops outside the base. This makes it particularly valuable for a quick withdrawal: a complete recall is unnecessary for this route.

### Procedure

1. Before attacking, check which server you are on and which transfer is available as an emergency exit.
2. Attack the planned target.
3. Watch the map and incoming marches.
4. In clear danger, immediately choose the appropriate direction:
   - **Opposing server → own server:** **Return**.
   - **Own server → opposing server:** **Alliance Competition → Raid → Go**.
5. Confirm server travel and select the new position where the game offers it.
6. Immediately check troop displays, return routes and incoming marches after traveling.
7. Resume fighting, heal or regroup only after reassessing the situation.

### Advantages

- No prior recall of all troops required.
- Free according to documented player experience.
- Depending on your starting side, the server boundary can serve as an emergency exit in either direction.
- According to documented experience, the destination on the opposing server and your new home position when returning can both be freely chosen.

### Limitations

- Available only while the relevant Raid is open and travel is permitted.
- Check combat status, Battle Frenzy and special rules in the current confirmation dialog.
- How every already active friendly or enemy march is handled after the jump is not fully documented publicly.
- Raid’s destination server is not automatically the same as the opponent in another simultaneous event. Check the destination and return route before confirming.

The complete outward and return journey is described in Tip #9 – Relocate for free through Raid.

## Escape route B: Recall troops and use a Random Relocator

The Random Relocator moves your base to a position on the map’s edge that you cannot freely select. It is less convenient than a targeted teleport but works as an inexpensive prepared emergency exit.

For this procedure, recall your troops to the base first. Only then use the relocator.

### Procedure

1. Stop further attacks immediately.
2. Recall all your teams and marches to your base.
3. Check whether the opponent has already hit or the battle is underway.
4. Open the **Random Relocator**.
5. Confirm the teleport before the battle starts.
6. After jumping, check your new position, opponents, shield and troops.
7. If the edge position is poor, do not reflexively spend more valuable relocators; assess the new danger first.

### Advantages

- Available independently of Raid if you have an item and teleporting is permitted.
- Cheaper than an Advanced Relocator.
- Random relocation makes it harder for the opponent to continue immediately at your previous location.

### Limitations

- All required troops must return in time.
- The destination cannot be planned and may be far from the alliance or event target.
- Once a battle is underway, it is too late for this teleport.
- An incoming march is no license to wait: the shorter its remaining time, the greater the risk of missing the teleport window.

More properties of the three relocators are covered in Tip #4 – Teleport correctly.

## Which escape route fits?

| Situation | Preferred route | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Danger after attacking on the opposing server | **Return** | quick transfer to your own server |
| Danger after fighting on your own server, for example during Capital Conquest | **Raid → Go** | quick transfer to the opposing server |
| Server travel available, troops still marching | **Raid jump** | no complete recall needed |
| Raid unavailable, all troops home | **Random Relocator** | prepared, inexpensive emergency exit |
| Exact new position required | **Advanced Relocator** | only this regular relocator allows targeted placement |
| Battle already underway | **no normal relocator** | withdrawal began too late |
| Escape route unclear or blocked | **do not attack** | Hit & Run without the Run is merely a risky attack |

## Hit & Run during Capital Conquest

For a small alliance, Hit & Run can be a useful alternative when coordinated objective fighting with fixed raid teams is unrealistic. Prepared raid teams have the greatest influence on the capital and batteries; anyone unable to supply enough active or strong players for that can instead:

- attack only clearly approved, lightly protected targets,
- launch brief scoring or pressure-relieving attacks,
- recognize strong counterattacks early,
- relocate the base in time through a Raid jump or relocator,
- preserve troops and deploy them again after withdrawing.

This tactic does not replace server-wide operational planning. However, it prevents a small alliance from consuming all its troops in a position it realistically cannot hold.

Before the event, decide clearly:

- **Objective plan:** Support the capital and batteries together with the server.
- **Hit & Run plan:** Launch mobile attacks, occupy opponents, pursue personal goals in a controlled way and limit your losses.

Switch between these plans only on instruction. Otherwise players are missing at the objective while others assume shared support.

## Signals to stop

Consider withdrawing no later than when one of these signals appears:

- A much stronger player teleports immediately nearby.
- A rally flag appears on your base.
- Several enemy marches start simultaneously.
- Your troops are already heavily damaged.
- The planned escape route may close.
- Operational leadership calls for a change of area or plan.

Not every scouting action automatically means an attack. Nevertheless, with a large power difference, reacting too early is often better than reacting one second too late.

## Common mistakes

- **Attacking without a prepared escape route:** Hit & Run becomes an ordinary risky attack.
- **Waiting for impact:** Normal relocators do not work during an ongoing battle.
- **Unnecessarily recalling all troops for a Raid jump:** Current DIE experience indicates this is unnecessary.
- **Leaving troops outside when using the Random Relocator:** This procedure recalls them to the base first.
- **Starting “one more attack”:** Additional seconds may cost the safe withdrawal.
- **Confusing the Raid destination with the event opponent:** Check the target server before every cross-server jump.
- **Failing to check after teleporting:** Immediately review troop routes, incoming marches, shield and new surroundings.
- **Confusing Hit & Run with constant fleeing:** After relocating, regroup, heal and prepare the next controlled deployment.
- **Leaving the objective group without agreement:** Mobile tactics and the objective plan must be assigned beforehand.

## Limitations and open questions

- Raid jumping with troops outside the base relies on current DIE player experience and the already documented Tip #9.
- Handling of active friendly and enemy marches after cross-server travel is not yet documented for every case.
- The Random Relocator is evidenced by Tip #4; which states besides an ongoing battle prevent its use still needs testing.
- Teleport rules, cooldowns, target servers and overlapping events may change with updates.
- A new location is not automatically safe. Reassess the map after every jump.

## Sources and reliability

- **DIE practical knowledge:** The combination of Raid jumping when threatened and recalling troops before using a Random Relocator comes from current alliance player experience.
- **Related DIE tips:** Tip #4 documents regular relocators’ effects and combat restriction; Tip #9 documents free cross-server jumping and its use with troops outside the base.
