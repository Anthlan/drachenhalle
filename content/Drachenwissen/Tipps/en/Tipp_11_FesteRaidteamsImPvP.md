# 💡 Tip #11 – Fixed raid teams in PvP

## Key points

- A **raid team** is organized around one responsible rally leader and four assigned participants.
- Fixed groups are the optimal rally format: everyone knows the target, team, start signal, stop signal and their responsibility.
- The rally leader does more than open the rally. They check the target and timing, specify the formation and decide after battle whether to repeat, rotate or withdraw.
- The four participants keep their agreed PvP team and one march slot available, join immediately and do not change their lineup on their own.
- At least one substitute per raid team prevents the entire process from stopping when a member is unavailable.
- A spontaneously filled rally can also be strong and win. Without fixed responsibilities, filling usually takes longer, team quality varies and follow-up attacks are harder to plan.
- Briefly review the battle report after every important attack. Then change only one thing where possible.

> **Remember:** One call, five clear roles, one shared target.

## What is a fixed raid team?

A fixed raid team is a prepared group for PvP rallies. These five people are committed to working together for the operation:

1. **one rally leader**, responsible for opening and directing the attack,
2. **four fixed participants**, who join immediately with agreed teams.

The five-person group is an **alliance organizational model**. It defines clear responsibilities, not necessarily a universal technical limit of the game. If current rally capacity or an event shows different values, the in-game display always takes priority.

Tip #2 explains basic rally mechanics. This tip builds on it and answers the organizational question: how does a possible rally become a fast, repeatable PvP procedure?

## Why fixed teams work better

With a spontaneous rally, organization often only begins after it starts:

- Who is online?
- Who has a march slot free?
- Which team should join?
- Is the formation suitable for this target?
- Who substitutes for an unavailable member?
- Who opens the next attack?

These questions cost time. The opponent can reinforce, heal, teleport or prepare a counterattack.

A fixed raid team answers these questions before deployment. This creates four advantages:

| Advantage | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Speed** | The rally fills after the signal without a lengthy search. |
| **Predictability** | The group’s strength, heroes and approximate troop count are known. |
| **Responsibility** | One person decides; four know what is expected of them. |
| **Repeatability** | After reviewing the report and healing, the same group can quickly deploy again. |

For maximum effect, fixed raid teams are therefore the best application of rally tactics.

## The five roles

### 1. Rally leader

The rally leader has operational responsibility. They:

- check the target, garrison and enemy activity,
- coordinate the attack with operational leadership,
- specify the approved team or desired team type,
- start only when participants and substitutes are reachable,
- give start, stop and repeat signals,
- read the battle report afterward, and
- announce whether to heal, rotate or attack again.

The strongest player is often a good rally leader, but strength alone is insufficient. Reliability, awareness and clear communication are equally important.

### 2 through 5. Fixed participants

Each participant:

- keeps one march slot free at the agreed time,
- uses the pre-agreed PvP team,
- joins only the approved rally,
- does not change heroes or formation without consultation,
- immediately reports insufficient troops, healing readiness or online time,
- waits for the next instruction after attacking.

A weaker but reliable participant is often more valuable than a stronger player who responds only after several calls.

### Substitute

A substitute is not one of the five fixed places, but knows the same procedure. They step in when someone is unavailable, still healing or unable to free a march slot in time.

Without a pre-assigned substitute, precisely the delay that fixed raid teams aim to avoid occurs.

## The raid card before battle

A short, copyable operational card is enough for each raid team:

```text
RAID TEAM A
Rally leader: [Name]
Participants: [Name 1] / [Name 2] / [Name 3] / [Name 4]
Substitute: [Name]
Primary target: [Target or target type]
Team requirement: [Main team / faction / approved formation]
Start signal: [short word or symbol]
Stop signal: [short word or symbol]
Coordination channel: [Alliance chat / separate chat]
```

Shorter, clearer signals reduce misunderstandings during battle.

## Optimal attack procedure

### 1. Approve the target

Operational leadership or the rally leader marks exactly one target. No second person simultaneously starts a competing rally with the same participants.

### 2. Confirm readiness

The four fixed participants briefly confirm. Anyone not ready reports this immediately; the substitute takes over.

### 3. Start the rally and fill immediately

The rally leader starts. The four assigned players join with the agreed team. Other members do not occupy reserved places without being asked.

### 4. Observe impact and counterreaction

During the attack, operational leadership watches reinforcements, incoming marches and possible counterattacks. The raid team does not begin another action in parallel.

### 5. Read the report

After impact, check at least:

- soldiers deployed and lost,
- enemy formation and faction counters,
- damage dealt, damage received and healing,
- visible development differences,
- in base defense, a possible breakthrough and additional deaths on the training ground.

Tip #12 – Read battle reports correctly explains the full review.

### 6. Give a clear follow-up decision

The rally leader gives exactly one next action:

- **REPEAT** – same group, same target,
- **ADJUST** – test one specified change,
- **ROTATE** – the next raid team takes over,
- **HEAL** – the group restores readiness,
- **STOP** – the target or situation does not justify another attack.

## Organize several raid teams

Large, active alliances should form several fixed teams rather than overload one group:

| Group | Possible task |
| --- | --- |
| **Raid team A** | break the strongest target or main enemy garrison |
| **Raid team B** | follow-up attack, counterattack or second objective |
| **Raid team C** | reserve, relief or protection of important players |

Operational leadership assigns targets and prevents groups from taking one another’s participants. During Capital Conquest, for example, one team can be assigned to the capital and others to priority batteries.

## If a complete five-person group is unavailable

A fixed five-person group is optimal, but not every alliance can fully field one at all times. Then:

1. name available core players,
2. still state the target and team requirement clearly,
3. seek the missing participants specifically before starting,
4. allow more time for filling and checking,
5. after attacking, reconfirm who is ready for the next rally.

Such a rally can still be very strong. It is simply slower and less reliably repeatable. The goal remains to gradually form fixed raid teams from recurring spontaneous participants.

If even this lacks sufficient strength or activity and a position cannot be held, Tip #10 – Hit & Run in PvP is the more suitable alternative.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting the rally before finding participants:** The opponent gets unnecessary reaction time.
- **No fixed leader:** Multiple instructions create competing targets and empty rallies.
- **Participants changing teams on their own:** Planned overall strength and roles break down.
- **Assigning strong players to several groups:** Two raid teams simultaneously rely on the same person.
- **Naming no substitute:** One absence stops the entire process.
- **Blindly repeating after victory:** The opponent may have changed formation, garrison or reinforcements.
- **Changing everything after defeat:** The next report cannot show which adjustment actually helped.
- **Putting personal goals ahead of the group commitment:** A committed participant must keep their march slot available for the raid team.

## Compact checklist

### Before deployment

- [ ] Rally leader named
- [ ] four fixed participants confirmed
- [ ] at least one substitute assigned
- [ ] primary and alternative targets defined
- [ ] approved team or formation agreed
- [ ] start and stop signals known
- [ ] march slots and troops available
- [ ] healing and withdrawal prepared

### During deployment

- [ ] only the responsible rally leader starts
- [ ] fixed participants fill immediately
- [ ] substitute joins only when instructed
- [ ] enemy reinforcements and counter-marches reported
- [ ] exactly one follow-up decision made after the report
