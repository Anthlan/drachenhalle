# 💡 Tip #1 – Move troops efficiently

## Key points

- **Prepare an attack:** Send the troop to a resource tile near the target first, then launch the actual attack from there.
- **Reduce reaction time:** The final attack route becomes shorter, giving the opponent less time to react. This does not give the troop a speed bonus.
- **Keep fighting immediately:** After an attack, a troop that is still combat-ready can attack its next target straight away.
- **Retreat faster:** After the attack, march toward a nearby resource tile and immediately press “Retreat”. This uses the faster resource-tile march speed for the return journey.
- **Remember healing:** Wounded soldiers can only be healed after returning to your own base.

> **Remember:** Resource tiles shorten the crucial journey – both when attacking and retreating.

## What is this tip for?

This tip mainly applies to planned attacks on more distant targets. Instead of attacking directly from your base, you use a resource tile as a **staging point or waypoint**. The advantage comes from a better starting position for the actual attack, rather than increased march speed.

It helps you:

- significantly shorten the final attack route,
- give the target less time to react,
- attack several nearby targets in succession, and
- move your troop flexibly around the map after an attack or bring it home faster.

## How the waypoint tactic works

1. **Choose a target:** Decide which base or other target you want to attack.
2. **Find a resource tile:** Choose a tile as close as possible to the target that can serve as an unobtrusive staging point.
3. **Send the troop ahead:** March to the resource tile first, rather than directly to the actual target.
4. **Prepare the attack:** Watch the march and check the target, march time and troop strength again before attacking.
5. **Attack from the waypoint:** Launch the attack only when close to the target. One variant described by the community redirects the march to the actual target shortly before reaching the resource tile.
6. **Decide what comes next:** Attack another nearby target, or return the troop to your base if wounded soldiers need healing.

## Why the tactic works

### Shorter reaction time rather than higher speed

A resource tile does not automatically increase march speed. After pre-positioning, the troop is simply closer to the target. This shortens the **final attack route**, and the opponent has less time to respond to the specific attack.

While the troop is only marching toward a resource tile, it is not on a direct attack march against the target base. However, the troop remains visible on the world map. The method therefore **does not make a march invisible**; initially, it only conceals the actual intention and shortens the window between issuing the attack command and impact.

### Attack several targets without returning home

After a battle, the troop does not necessarily have to return to its own base first. If it remains combat-ready, it can attack the next target directly from its current position. This saves extra travel, particularly when several targets are in the same area.

> **Important:** Wounded troops can only be healed after the troop returns to your own base. After each battle, check whether another attack is really worth the time saved.

### Use a resource tile for a faster retreat

Resource tiles are useful for more than approaching a target. After an attack, you can also use them to bring the troop back to your base faster:

1. Select a **resource tile near the troop’s current position** after the attack.
2. Send the troop toward that tile.
3. Immediately after the march starts, press **“Retreat”**. The troop does not have to reach the resource tile first.
4. The return march then uses the resource-tile march speed rather than the slower speed of a direct march to the base.

You do not need a resource tile near your own base. What matters is a tile near the troop that is easy to reach, allowing you to start the resource march and cancel it immediately.

> **Practical benefit:** This gets wounded soldiers back to the hospital faster and makes the troop available sooner. Briefly compare the displayed return time before confirming, since research, bonuses and game updates can affect these times.

## Example from the illustration

| Comparison | March time | Converted |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Direct attack | 5:54 min | 354 seconds |
| Attack from the resource tile | 2:44 min | 164 seconds |
| Time saved on the attack route | **3:10 min** | **190 seconds** |

The shorter attack route therefore takes around **53.7% less time**:

`(354 − 164) ÷ 354 × 100 = 53.7%`

In other words, the wait from the attack command onward is only around **46.3%** of the direct march time. In this example, the direct attack takes about **2.16 times as long**. The troop’s actual march speed does not change.

> **Context:** This calculation compares the direct attack with an attack **from a resource tile already reached**. It excludes the time spent pre-positioning. For a single spontaneous attack, the overall journey may therefore be just as long or even longer. The tactical benefit mainly comes from reducing the opponent’s reaction time, positioning early, or launching several consecutive attacks.

## When is the method worthwhile?

| Situation | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| The attack is planned in advance | **Very useful:** You can move the troop close to the target in good time without issuing a direct attack command. |
| Several targets are close together | **Very useful:** After the first battle, the troop can move straight on. |
| The opponent should have as little reaction time as possible | **Useful:** The final route, and therefore the specific warning window, becomes shorter. |
| The troop should return quickly after the attack | **Very useful:** March toward a nearby resource tile and immediately select “Retreat”. |
| You only want a single immediate attack | **Compare first:** Pre-positioning plus the final route may take longer overall than the direct journey. |
| The troop already has many wounded soldiers | **Prefer returning:** Healing is impossible without returning to the base. |

## Practical checklist

- Is the resource tile really closer to the target than your current position?
- Is the tile free, and can the troop arrive there safely?
- How long is the final route from the resource tile to the target?
- Is the opponent active and likely to notice the march on the map?
- Is the remaining troop strength sufficient for the attack and a possible second target?
- Does the troop need to return for healing afterward, or can it keep fighting?
- Is there a resource tile near the troop’s current position for the retreat?
- Is the return via “march toward resource tile → immediate retreat” actually faster than going directly to the base?

## Limitations and open questions

- This tactic **does not provide invisibility**. Attentive players can spot the march on the map and guess your intention.
- Once the actual attack starts, assume the target may receive a warning. The exact warning display and timing should be checked in the game again after major updates.
- March times depend on distance, research, active bonuses and the troop used. The times of 5:54 and 2:44 minutes are a specific practical example, not universal values.
- The resource tile serves as a positioning point. If it becomes occupied, cleared or otherwise unusable, you need an alternative waypoint.
- The retreat variant also relies on the currently observed in-game calculation. Recheck the displayed march times after game updates.
